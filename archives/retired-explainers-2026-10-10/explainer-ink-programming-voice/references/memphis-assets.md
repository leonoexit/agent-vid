# Corporate Memphis assets for an Ink lesson

This is the user's new direction for explanatory illustrations inside the existing Ink skill. The writing hand is a separate photoreal cutout; do not apply Memphis stylization to it. Use light paper and handwritten navy type as the stable setting; add flat editorial figures/props with simplified geometry, organic silhouettes, occasional exaggerated limbs and restrained asymmetry. Keep one coherent family across the video. Plan where that family helps in the middle and conclusion as well as the hook: reuse a prop, crop into its relevant region, or generate a purposeful companion asset. A single generic opener followed by text-only mechanism pages is not sufficient visual planning. Native diagrams remain preferable to generated text for exact logic. Avoid corporate stock-art filler: each person, object or scene must explain a concrete situation, serve as a recurring anchor or make a useful relationship visible.

## Palette and roles

Navy #1a237e anchors the family. Suggested illustration accents: periwinkle #7b8cde, peach #efb99c, teal #4eaaa0 and warm yellow #f2ce63, with paper #fffefa. Pick a subset per asset; red correction/yellow marker retain their annotation roles. These are our design choices, not a universal Corporate Memphis specification. Prefer matte flat fills and a few confident curves; no glossy 3D, dramatic gradients, photoreal environments or mandatory blue cross-hatching.

- **Object/icon:** recognizable book, folder, ticket, container or device; strong silhouette, minimal detail.
- **Recurring anchor:** the same figure/prop retains pose family, proportions and palette; use the approved image as reference for variants.
- **Small scene:** one useful relation, such as a person reusing a nearby stored answer instead of visiting the source again. Leave room for exact labels and avoid implying a metaphor is the literal computation.

Do not force people into every technical concept. Native code, counters, results and arrows are better for exact changing evidence. Generate separate cutouts for parts that need independent motion; a single bitmap does not have movable internal limbs. Use transparency for cutouts and remove the old multiply blending/blue frame treatment when it muddies the colors. The updated template shows images in their original colors.
