# Familiar formats, visible explanations

Scope: one concise, researched question about an everyday object, technology, design or cultural history. Use familiar faceless formats instead of creating a new brand/content strategy for every request. Broader lists or many-topic compilations need another brief.

Choose the smallest adequate structure:

- Origin: familiar object → earlier constraint/context → decisive development → what survives today.
- Curiosity: recognizable observation → a question → evidence/mechanism → useful or surprising implication.
- Misconception: a plausible interpretation → a concrete test or source → corrected distinction → return to the original situation.

These are choices, not a fixed scene count. Hook with an intelligible question and visible subject. No mandatory shock claim, invented conflict or engagement-bait CTA. End on the evidence that earns the conclusion.

## Cold-entry hook

Assume a viewer meets the film in a feed without the post title, channel context or sound. Build two things together: recognition of the subject and a specific question, contrast or consequence worth following. Beauty, movement and a question mark alone do not supply either.

- Make the opening frame useful: show the subject in a recognizable context, or name it clearly in integrated scene typography. Avoid an empty fade-in, a logo/category preamble, or an entrance sequence that postpones the subject. Supporting pieces can still arrive progressively.
- Make the curiosity legible in the first beat, roughly the first 1–2 seconds as an editorial target, not a guaranteed retention threshold. State what is unusual, unresolved or useful about this particular subject. Do not spend those seconds merely announcing that an explanation is coming.
- Give the image/action and opening words complementary jobs. For an ambiguous icon, demonstrate its familiar use before isolating its geometry. An unsupported symbol can be mistaken for the player's own control; an unexplained close-up can be mistaken for decoration.
- Open a gap in the explanation, not in basic identification. “Why does this familiar thing work this way?” can withhold the answer while making the thing and the question clear. Do not force every topic into a mystery or invent surprising claims to obtain a hook.
- Start delivering evidence in the next beat. The hook must promise what the sourced film actually explains; do not imply a known inventor when the evidence only supports a convention or mechanism.

Worked correction, not a required template: a floating triangle with “SAO LẠI TAM GIÁC?” did not establish the topic clearly in the Play trial. A phone's visible media control paired with “NÚT PLAY / SAO LÀ TAM GIÁC?” identifies the subject and the question immediately. A press can make the progress bar move, then carry that triangle into the tape explanation. The opening voice could say “Vì sao nút phát nhạc lại có hình tam giác?” Choose a different opening device when another subject calls for it.

Before extending the film, inspect its first frame and approximately first three seconds at phone size, muted and without surrounding post text. Describe what a newcomer can identify and what they would expect to learn, using only what is already visible. Scene graphics and authored type must carry this; subtitles are supporting access, not the sole source of context. Then review with narration for duplication, delayed evidence and timing. If identification or the reason to continue is unclear, change wording/context/staging before adding more animation. Record an editorial judgment and any review limits; this check is not a measured retention result.

## Typography and images share the explanation

Begin with the sourced question and causal sequence. Plan narration, imagery and text hierarchy together. Decide what leads each passage: an image/action, an expressive phrase, or their combination. Typography supplies a question, contrast, emphasis or takeaway; images establish the subject, support evidence and show changes. Neither medium has to carry every claim by itself.

A short “VÌ SAO Ở LẠI?” can orient a passage while a typewriter and typing gesture develop the answer. “Đổi máy: dễ. Đổi thói quen: khó hơn.” can be an effective compact payoff when the images connect it to the example. Names, dates and quantities remain useful labels, but they are not the only permitted text. Judge copy by clarity, phone readability and hierarchy rather than minimum word count. Avoid transcribing the entire narration onto cards or placing an identical heading block in every shot.

Keep a recognizable object through meaningful changes and generate a missing detail or view when it clarifies the explanation. A new photograph is not automatically better than a strong existing image with concise text. Do not force abstract claims into obscure pictorial metaphors. Qualify disputed mechanisms instead of inventing a literal demonstration. Captions remain separate from authored scene text.

For each passage, record the leading element, what the image demonstrates, what the text contributes and how their relationship changes with the spoken clause. Some passages can have no content text; others can be led by expressive typography. There is no target text/image ratio and no requirement to explain the whole film through images alone.

Write the shot score as: what viewers know → spoken claim and visible transformation → what is now understood → evidence carried forward. Select a collage verb that fits: separate to distinguish, uncover to explain, assemble to connect, carry across contexts to show continuity, enlarge to inspect. A reveal is useful only if it exposes information; a moving label is not automatically a mechanism. Preserve sufficient context and do not reveal every outcome before its narration.

Research with authoritative sources, preferably museum collections, original records or direct institutional explanations. Save claim-level URLs and qualification notes in sources.md. If experts dispute a causal story, say so; do not choose the more entertaining version as settled fact. A generated period object is an illustration, not an archival photograph or a precise reconstruction. Do not invent quotes, inscriptions, dates or authentic-looking evidence.

Adapt source material into original wording and an authored visual sequence. Familiar subject matter is welcome; copying another creator's script or unlicensed footage is not the production method. For time-sensitive claims, verify current information. General explainers do not become personalized medical/legal/financial advice.

Keep the voice connected, curious and matter-of-fact. Let wording explain why the visible change matters. Shorten repetitive passages before speeding up speech. A reading hold should name what viewers inspect; decorative drift should not fill an empty argument.

During a held explanation, keep the current subject as an anchor and introduce only the evidence/name/detail needed by the next clause. Use the difference between a deliberate story action and subtle material jitter; only the former advances the explanation. Avoid a new full layout for every sentence, and avoid unrelated arrows or diagram fragments added merely to fill space. See [Rosetta motion](rosetta-motion.md).
