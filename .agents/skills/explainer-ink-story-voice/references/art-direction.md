# Handwritten ink on white paper

Source: main batch pages, not alternate demo A–F directions. Preserve the informal handwritten lesson, generous paper and editorial hierarchy. This differs from the existing parchment/robot business skill: brighter paper, typography as the primary subject, less character-driven staging.

## Tokens and type

- Paper `#fffefa` / white, ink `#1a237e`, correction `#c0392b`, body `#30343b`, secondary `#697079`, yellow wash `rgba(255,235,59,.42)`.
- Sriracha headings 84–112 px on 1080 px canvas; Mali body 44–54 px, line height 1.35–1.5; monospace code 36–42 px. These are starting ranges: shorten or split dense copy before shrinking below comfortable phone reading size.
- Headings typically 2–4 short lines. A main page typically carries one claim plus 2–3 short supporting units. Code stays compact. Do not enforce word counts independent of actual layout.
- Use left alignment for reasoning and comparison; centered type for a deliberate question or landing statement. Slight rotation (about 1°) may distinguish a heading or note, never long text/code.
- Fine paper grain stays static and faint. No aged brown wash, glass material, neon gradients, plastic assets or heavy black offset shadows.

## Layout grammar for 1080×1920

Recompose the source's square pages for portrait; do not stretch or letterbox them. Keep about 90 px side margins, a quiet top margin and a dedicated bottom caption area. Main reading area ends around y=1550; captions around y=1650–1810. The supplied CSS is a starting layout, not proof that arbitrary copy fits.

**Statement:** small context line, large handwritten proposition, a brief supporting line. Let type dominate.

**Comparison:** common question above two clearly labelled notes. Stack vertically when the labels or content are long; use side-by-side only when readable. Compare the same attribute on both sides.

**Example:** question or claim, short code/data excerpt, exact result, then annotation explaining the link. Keep output visually distinct from code. Never animate a result before its causal input is established.

**Illustration:** one image in an imperfect blue frame, surrounded by a short premise and explanatory caption. Usually 25–40% of the picture area; do not make it a generic full-screen wallpaper. Allow an intentionally larger image when its detail is the subject.

**Rule:** strong conclusion with one limiting condition beneath, selective yellow marker. No forced follow/subscribe outro.

Lines and borders: blue 2–4 px, occasional doubled irregular contour, rounded corners, faint blue shadow. Doodles: a purposeful arrow, bracket, circle or question mark in the same ink. Avoid a dense icon set or using boxes around every sentence.

## Generated illustrations

Prompt for the actual semantic role, not merely a style:

“Blue ballpoint pen illustration on near-white lightly textured paper, loose confident hand-drawn contours and restrained cross-hatching, generous empty margins, [subject and meaningful relationship], simple legible silhouette, occasional tiny red annotation accent, editorial learning notebook. No rendered words, letters, numbers, code, watermark, 3D plastic, polished vector icon or photorealistic background.”

Keep the image's blue ink/light direction/paper compatible with the page. Use native labels outside the bitmap. Generated art is an analogy or illustration, not a scientific diagram unless manually checked. Save the prompt, intended role and output path in the project asset notes. Review actual pixels and crop at phone scale. Reference images establish style; do not recycle their AI subject matter into unrelated topics.

## Motion vocabulary

Reveal a phrase or note over roughly .25–.45 s with a small upward settle. Sweep a marker or draw one underline at the spoken cue. Keep both comparison terms present while explaining their difference. A brief page dissolve/cut is enough to change context. Do not type every character, keep the paper drifting or force continuous motion to pass an action-density metric. Reading is intentional activity here.
