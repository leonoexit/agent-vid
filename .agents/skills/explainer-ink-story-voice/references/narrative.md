# Text-led explanatory reasoning

The story's anchor is a question, a distinction or a concrete example. It need not be a persistent physical object. Keep the viewer oriented by repeating a small piece of language or data, not by pinning the same dashboard on every scene.

A useful progression is question → plausible intuition → example/evidence → explanation → limit/takeaway. Treat this as a reasoning aid, not mandatory scenes. Sometimes the example should come first; sometimes a paired definition resolves the question immediately. Avoid manufacturing a false belief just to refute it.

Each beat has three jobs recorded in the storyboard:
- **Viewer question:** what uncertainty does this beat resolve?
- **Screen evidence:** what exact sentence, code, result, comparison or illustration makes it understandable?
- **Spoken connection:** why does that evidence support the next idea?

Also record reveal cues and the reason for any image. A heading alone is rarely evidence. “Cache is fast” becomes explainable with one request, a stored answer and the next request reusing it. If the price changes while the cached response remains unchanged, show both labelled prices before concluding it can be stale. Do not imply a TTL guarantees consistency.

## Voice versus page

Write connected spoken prose. Let screen copy compress the conclusion rather than duplicate a paragraph of narration. An exact code fragment, a contrast or an important definition can be read verbatim when useful. No theatrical silence between every punchline. Keep enough dwell for a full glance after the last relevant reveal.

For novices, introduce technical names at the moment the example gives them meaning. For experienced viewers, emphasize the boundary case or tradeoff. Do not turn a broad topic into a rushed survey: pick one useful question and state its scope.

Good emphasis changes understanding: underline “reuses”, contrast “stored price” with “current price”, cross out a precisely false claim and supply its correction. Color, circles and arrows should identify a relationship. Avoid random word-by-word kinetic text, flickering highlights or decorating every line.

## Composition and illustration

A large statement gives one proposition weight. A pair of labelled notes makes a distinction. A code/result page provides testable evidence. An illustration provides a memorable referent or analogy. A short rule states what generalizes and where it stops. Choose freely among these roles, repeating a role when it helps.

Use generated art where prose alone demands unnecessary imagination: a coherent metaphor, recognizable setting or difficult-to-draw subject. Explain the mapping and its limits. Do not put a robot next to every abstract term. For exact logic, use native labels and simple diagrams instead of image-generated pseudo-code.

The source uses suspense, rhetorical questions and dramatic claims. Adopt curiosity and readable contrast, not unsupported certainty. For external factual claims, verify sources as appropriate; do not treat the reference's AI assertions as an evidence base.

## Review the argument

Read only the narration: does it remain connected? View only the page sequence: are the question, evidence and conclusion identifiable? View them together: does the needed evidence appear before its consequence is asserted, and remain long enough to compare? These checks complement one another; silent pages need not teach every detail independently.
