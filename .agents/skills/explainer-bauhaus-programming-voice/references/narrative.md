# Let geometry expose the rule

The reference makes an argument through sharp contrast, scale, subtraction and quantified forms. For programming education, replace its slogan/manifesto cadence with a concrete question whose answer becomes visible. The narrator calmly points to what changes and explains why it matters; firm headlines do not justify oversimplified claims.

## Choose a route, not a phase checklist

- **Construct and vary:** start with a small input, group/order/route it under a rule, inspect the result, then alter one input to establish what remains invariant. Fits remainder, indexing, queues, sets and simple data transformations.
- **Matched contrast:** use the same task and input for two mechanisms; align their corresponding parts, expose one difference and show its consequence. Fits copy/reference, equality/identity, print/return. Avoid simultaneously introducing two unfamiliar systems.
- **Remove a mistaken interpretation:** name a plausible confusion, execute a small counterexample, replace only the false part of the mental model. Fits “return prints on screen” or “assignment is a live link.” Strike-through is an editorial conclusion, not proof by itself.

Combine or choose another route when needed. One concept and one connected example remain the scope; scene count and order follow the explanation. A second input is often useful but not mandatory. Do not invent mistakes or turn all topics into sorting dots. Complex recursion or ownership may need labeled containers and native code rather than decorative abstraction.

## Write the visual argument alongside narration

For each meaningful clause record: viewer uncertainty, exact phrase, current native state, operation, result state, what must remain visible, and where attention goes next. Also name what each shape means and where the metaphor stops. Stable colors/IDs help carry an object across a reframe; do not keep the entire layout frozen just to preserve continuity.

Use a short headline to establish the task. Let code or geometry take over when it becomes evidence. Say the action during its motion, then explain its result while that result is legible. Introduce unfamiliar notation at its first necessary use. Remove duplicate setup before speeding the voice. Reading pauses are allowed for a stated comparison or prediction; do not fill arbitrary durations with decorative movement.

If a token means one item, keep its count conserved across a grouping or explicitly explain creation/deletion. If a width encodes quantity, scale it consistently or label it schematic. A triangle is not automatically a condition, a circle is not automatically a function and a color is not automatically an error. Label relationships before relying on them.

## Worked native example: remainder

Hook: `7 % 3` asks for what remains after full groups. Seven visible dots form two complete groups of three, leaving one. Preserve these groups, add one dot to make eight, observe two remaining. Add one more to make nine and form a third full group; the remainder becomes zero. Conclude that `%` returns the leftover count in this nonnegative-integer/positive-divisor example, not the group count.

The scene shift may preserve seven/eight existing items in their established positions. Do not replay a known outcome as if it were a new computation. A zero remainder needs an empty labeled remainder area, not a vanished graphic with an unexplained zero. Do not extend this example's rule to negative operands without checking the chosen language's semantics.

Starter headings and cues are editable demonstrations. For another topic, replace both the data and the geometry; changing `%` to another title over the same grouping animation is not a new explanation.

## Review as a viewer

Can the question be understood before the rule is named? Does each inference have visible evidence? Could someone tell which values changed with captions hidden? Can a viewer hear the narration alone as connected prose? Does the last sentence answer the opening question with the necessary boundary? These determine success; a tally of shapes, motion events or layouts does not.
