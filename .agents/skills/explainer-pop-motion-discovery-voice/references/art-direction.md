# The reference's visual grammar

The supplied square grid is an editorial/event-poster reference. Adapt its visual grammar to one sequential vertical video; do not reproduce the grid, event campaign, Portuguese wording or registration CTAs.

- Saturated yellow #FFF064, purple #7546D9, pink #EDB1DA, orange #FF8C35, red #F04459 and cyan #83D9E9. Give each passage a dominant field plus one or two accents. Use dark purple #342046/near-black for readable ink, white paper for labels.
- Monochrome photographs and deliberately tinted photographic objects with narrow irregular white cut contours. These retain photographic detail and halftone texture. Avoid glossy 3D clay, generic vector icons and beige vintage scrapbook treatment.
- Two primary type voices: expressive handwritten/painted headings and clear sans-serif explanatory copy. Generated lettering can embody a key phrase. Editable Vietnamese display uses Corose for short headings; concise body copy and explanatory paper labels use JetBrains Mono, while captions and contextual application UI use Inter. Avoid serif body fonts. A paper surface does not automatically make its contents a heading. Preserve accents; never distort long body copy to imitate a poster.
- Foreground photo crosses a paper edge; a short strip can tilt; a major word may crop decoratively if meaning remains intact. Keep evidence and captions fully readable. Use a few strong relationships instead of a uniform UI card grid.
- Native dotted fields, thin waves, outline words and sparse doodles can supply rhythm. Their contrast stays subordinate to the evidence. A change in the argument may justify a field change; it does not require one. Avoid strobing colors or patterns.

Use alpha-following close paper shadows. Gen-img labels retain a contact shadow and a modest cast shadow rather than an opaque rectangular shadow. Set the photo/light direction consistently. Do not apply a thick border and heavy shadow twice when the generated asset already includes them.

The reference's foil texture, buildings, flowers and statues are optional motifs, not required assets. This version implements solid/patterned fields, tinted/monochrome cutouts, text strips, drawn marks and expressive type. It does not establish a generated-background workflow. Reference photos are not implied historical evidence.

## Choose fields for the film, not a scene-color cycle

The palette is a family of available inks, not an ordered template. Choose a dominant palette and field treatment for the particular film alongside its assets and shot score. Preserve the Pop identity through cutout treatment, typography and paper material; not every video needs yellow → purple → orange → pink → cyan, dotted texture, or the same torn side panel.

Make three decisions explicit in the storyboard:
- **Readability:** which fields separate the actual subject colors, preserve white cut edges and support readable type? Inspect generated assets on them. A pink subject may need a cooler or darker field; this is a compositional judgment, not a universal color meaning.
- **Story role:** is this a neutral stage, a distinction between two contexts, or an accent for an important reveal? A field can simply provide contrast and atmosphere. Do not invent symbolism after choosing a color. When color encodes a category/state, keep that mapping consistent within the film.
- **Continuity:** when is a new field useful, and what returns at the payoff? Keep the field through connected actions when it helps viewers track an object. Change at a meaningful shift or deliberate editorial beat, not because another voice section began. Use restrained transitions that maintain foreground contrast.

Solid fields, quiet dots/lines, cropped flat paper shapes and controlled split fields are alternatives, not a checklist. Choose the least busy support for the current composition. Do not reintroduce generated scenery/backgrounds or full-frame raster layouts through this guidance.

For a run of videos, vary the dominant palette, field proportions/pattern density and use of open space together with subject scale, image treatment and typography arrangement. Merely rotating hue behind an identical layout does not produce a new visual identity. If recent sibling projects are available, inspect a small sample of their opening/contact sheets for accidental repetition; retain deliberate series identity without copying their sequence. Do not require every video to be maximally different or use every color. Record one short reason for the selected treatment and any intentionally retained motif.

## Marks must earn their place

Native geometry is welcome when it makes a relationship readable. Do not add a free-floating arrow simply to bridge a composition, or use a pair of generic strokes as a substitute for a recognizable physical mechanism. In QWERTY, two crossing bars did not explain typebar jamming; the ending arrow added nothing to the already adjacent old/new devices. Both are removed in the revised trial. Prefer a meaningful crop/detail, purposeful alignment, a moved subject or a short label. If the narration only names a disputed hypothesis, a qualified label beside the relevant object is sufficient; do not stage a misleading pseudo-demonstration.

Use sparse hand marks when their target and reading function are clear (locating a feature, tracing an actual route, distinguishing a specific inscription). Match their weight and material to the collage. The Rosetta reference's annotations are contextual examples, not a mandate for doodles in every scene.

## Compose with typography and imagery

Use the approved image-led Pop Motion hierarchy: photographic cutout actions lead the explanation, short expressive type establishes the hook, and paper labels disambiguate entities or states. Let a concise payoff earn its place after the image demonstrates the distinction. Do not make every spoken idea a text panel. Preserve purposeful negative space rather than filling it with captions duplicated as headings.

Choose additional subjects, details or matching state variants when they let the viewer see a cause or result. Reuse a successful visual anchor through meaningful changes. The container bank supports labels; it is not the default structure for a scene. See [picture-beat planning](story.md#narration-is-the-thread-images-carry-the-visible-explanation).

Treat all authored scene components as physical collage pieces with their own held motion cadence, including native assets and standalone type. A paper label and its lettering, or a device and its controls, form one coherent moving assembly. Keep only the caption layer and global viewport outside this treatment; tune dense assemblies for readability. See [Rosetta motion](rosetta-motion.md).


## Materials and text containers

Build the visual idea from photographic cutouts, printed lettering, cut/torn paper and purposeful negative space. Choose the representation before coding its container. A generic icon plus a circular colored badge does not become Pop collage just by adding a white outline or shadow. Replacing rough SVG with stock icons and rounding boxes was an unsuccessful direction in the Save-icon trial; do not use that revision as the material baseline.

Physical subjects retain photographic detail: select a suitable library asset, sourced image or generated cutout. Use standalone expressive type when a container adds nothing. Give editorial labels a deliberate cut/torn-paper silhouette and close contact/cast shadow; vary shape only when it helps hierarchy. Preserve raster aspect ratios and registered safe text areas. Avoid both uniform sharp rectangular UI cards and uniform rounded cards/pills as the composition system. Slight irregularity should suggest a cut edge, not a noisy sawtooth effect around every word.

SVG/native geometry can explain an actual relation, locate a detail or preserve exact editable data. It should not be used to approximate a photographed device or fill missing storyboard assets. An interface symbol such as Save belongs in a recognizable toolbar/application fragment when its function is the point. Show the relevant context, crop for legibility and integrate that excerpt as a printed piece; do not enlarge a bare stock glyph and invent a decorative badge around it. Retain genuine source-interface geometry where necessary for recognition instead of restyling every UI corner. Circles occurring in the subject, print composition or a meaningful mark are not banned.

Captions remain stationary, readable and visually subordinate. Use a quiet paper-strip reading surface with subtly cut edges, ample padding and restrained shadow; avoid chat-bubble/pill styling or animated torn edges. The template's native cut-edge fallback supplies readability without requiring a new generated asset for every caption. Editorial labels can use actual raster paper backplates, and some words need no plate at all. Keep exact changing copy native and aligned with its paper parent.

Review the first explanation with actual assets at phone size. Does the composition read as a photographed/printed collage, or as an app dashboard decorated with stickers? If the latter, change the chosen representation, framing and hierarchy rather than just adjusting radius, border weight or shadows. Check text fit and overlaps again after replacing a container; do not stretch the raster or raise z-index to conceal collisions. A library image can be suitable even when its earlier arrangement in a video was not.

## Typography roles

Corose supplies expressive short headings, questions and occasional concise payoff headings. JetBrains Mono supplies concise explanatory copy, supporting facts and paper labels, restoring the pre-Corose body treatment. Inter supplies captions and contextual application UI. Avoid serif body fonts. Hierarchy comes from role, size, weight, spacing and contrast; do not set all text in Corose merely to make a unified handwritten style. Dates can use either face according to their display or metadata role. Generated lettering remains an independent expressive asset. Keep long or fast-changing text in the readable body family.

The template includes the DFVN Corose personal-use trial font and provenance in `assets/fonts/corose-provenance.json`. Do not imply this grants commercial rights. For commercial delivery use an appropriately licensed Corose supplied by the user, or explicitly disclose a fallback to the retained `display.ttf` (Sriracha); do not silently substitute and claim Corose. Check actual Vietnamese glyph rendering, wraps and plate fit after font changes. Corose may lack punctuation such as an ellipsis: the template explicitly falls back to bundled Inter.

Fictional reaction characters use the minimal hand-drawn stick-figure language specified in [reaction beats](motion-staging.md#visual-reaction-beats): few ink strokes, expressive posture, sparse color and ample negative space. The previous detailed comic character is not the target. Keep authentic sourced photographs for named historical people. A modern application interface can use its own recognizable window controls, typography and soft corners; it is an actual subject, not a generic label container. If showing an old mechanism in a modern-looking mockup, state the historical scope visibly and do not imply current software behaves that way.

## Opening heading accents

Keep Corose readable from the first frame and use a focused marker or paper swipe to emphasize the subject or the hook’s question. Choose an accent from the film’s palette with strong text contrast. In the approved film, a yellow swipe draws behind “API key”, a white underlay follows the question, and the question gives one small emphasis on the spoken reversal. Those colors, two accents and exact timings are examples, not a mandatory recipe.

Build editable text and its accent in one assembly: a relative inline span, an absolutely positioned accent behind it, and the lettering above. A mildly irregular native silhouette can suggest a marker/paper edge. Animate the accent’s scaleX from the left while the text stays fully visible; a short roughly 0.3–0.6 second wipe usually suffices. Anchor any later emphasis to the relevant spoken cue, then settle. Keep breathing room around Vietnamese accents and avoid hiding letters, shaking whole sentences, cycling glows or looping attention effects. Inspect first-frame legibility, the drawn state and maximum text movement at phone size. The approved project’s `.hook-accent` CSS and opening timeline demonstrate this optional implementation without adding another runtime dependency.
