# Composition, context and causal flow

Use the concept to choose environments. Input/interface establishes the problem; a data diagram explains the mechanism; an outcome scene shows the consequence; code recap connects the observation to syntax. A short lesson may need fewer contexts. Changing background color alone is not a new environment.

Carry a recognizable object, name, color or state across contexts. For example, the same age value can move from a form to a comparison to a gate response. Establish the destination before the crucial action. Keep both source and destination visible during a copy or transfer. Reframing can support focus but is not a quota or the identity of this style.

Compose an overview, close action and result comparison as different arrangements rather than permanently stacking title, code, diagram and captions. Let relevant code enter near its subject. Give the main asset enough scale; surrounding elements should explain a relationship rather than decorate empty space.

Readable states can be still. Use transitions to preserve continuity, not to avoid every moment of stillness. Keep moving geometry stable, scale with transforms where practical, and avoid abrupt replacement of large text blocks. Do not overlap two readable headlines in a dissolve. These are general craft lessons, not a separate motion-engine optimization program.

Review four questions: Can the viewer identify the subject? Can they see what it did? Does the result follow from that action? Does the visual treatment retain the reference's material, color and hierarchy? Assignment and if/else trials do not establish that one arrangement works for every programming topic.
